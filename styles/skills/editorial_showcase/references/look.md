# Visual kit

`W` and `H` denote output frame dimensions. Choose the kit once and keep it across the film.

## Palette

- Light surface: warm off-white, for example `#F3F0E8`.
- Dark surface: near-black, for example `#15171B`.
- One house accent, preferably from the supplied brand. A default starting point is blue `#354FD4` on light surfaces; use a lighter tonal version on dark surfaces when needed for contrast.
- Use neutral readable text when the accent fails contrast. Never preserve a dark blue/red word over a dark shirt merely because a reference did so.
- Actual portfolio, website and product media keep their own brand colours. This is not permission to give every overlay a new accent.
- Match neutral panels to the footage without strongly recolouring screenshots or product colours. Grain/paper texture is optional, subtle and confined to the intended paper scene.

## Two-family type kit

Choose one of these coherent pairs; do not mix both:

1. **Editorial + handwriting:** a legible serif family for headings and supporting captions, plus a handwriting family for a short accent only. Use a medium/semibold serif for small text, never hairline display type.
2. **Editorial + utility:** a serif family with a real italic face for headings/expressive accents, plus a sans family for supporting captions and UI. This is the fallback when handwriting is illegible, unavailable or incompatible with the script.

Possible starting families are Source Serif 4 (editorial), Caveat (handwriting) or Inter/Noto Sans (utility), subject to local availability, licence and actual glyph tests. Names are suggestions, not bundled assets or guaranteed language coverage. Check all letters, apostrophes and numbers in the actual transcript; do not let a silent font fallback introduce a third family.

The short expressive accent can be handwritten or italic; its role is fixed even when the font changes. Do not add a third font for a phone, paper scene, logo-like heading or CTA. A supplied logo is artwork, not a reason to restyle captions.

## Framing

- Keep the face and relevant hands clear. Do not place the editorial heading above the head when there is no room.
- Use one consistent supporting-caption zone per layout. Presenter, demo and full-screen paper are separate layouts; positions may adapt deliberately, not drift on every phrase.
- Default title inset is 10% of each edge, then further exclude the destination platform's UI. Preview the actual masks; generic margins are only a fallback.
- A three-row showcase is for the shape and breadth of work. Fine print, numbers and interface steps need a dedicated close-up or an approved readable label.
